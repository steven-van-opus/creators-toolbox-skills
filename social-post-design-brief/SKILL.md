# Social Post Design Brief

## Choose what the visual should prove

Read the supplied announcement and inspect the actual product assets. Write one line from the audience's point of view that states what changed, what they can do now, and what to click next. Keep the audience, the date or access limit, and the proof for any claim in the working brief. If a claim lacks proof, drop it from the public copy and record what would verify it.

Look for one image that makes the change visible. A relevant UI state, an annotated detail, or a before-and-after can each work. Crop out account details and use representative demo data. When a capture is unavailable, use a type-led layout and identify the exact capture still needed.

## Decide how many frames the idea earns

Use one card when the headline and one view explain the feature. Use a carousel when understanding depends on a sequence or distinct examples. Give each frame a job and one copy line. Put the release on the first frame and place the action beside the point where the reader understands enough to take it. Avoid asking readers to swipe just to learn what changed.

When the brief includes visual references, record the compositional decision each one supports, such as a large crop around one control. Apply it with the project's assets and brand.

## Hand off an executable brief

For each format, specify the canvas dimensions, exact on-image words, asset and crop, emphasis and alignment, and accessible description. Include the post caption and action destination if needed by the brief. Distinguish captured UI from diagrams or example illustrations.

Check the first frame at a phone feed width and in the expected thumbnail crop. If the main line or the proof disappears, enlarge it or remove competing detail before resizing. Remove any claim that exists only in the artwork and cannot be verified from the supplied launch material.
