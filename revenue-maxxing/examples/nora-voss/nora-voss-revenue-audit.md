# Nora Voss revenue audit

Example report for the revenue-maxxing skill. Nora Voss is fictional. Every figure is from the example brief, not from a real channel.

## 1. Snapshot

Nora already makes money from ads, occasional sponsors, and a $49 template. The next useful move is a small paid checklist that answers a question people already ask, not a membership or a course.

## 2. Income map

| Source | Confirmed in the brief | Recurring or one-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube ads, about $1,400 a month | Yes, as an example figure | Recurring, but it moves with views |
| Sponsored walkthroughs, $2,000 each, two to three times a quarter | Yes, as an example figure | One-off |
| $49 Notion template, about 30 sales a month | Yes, as an example figure | Recurring product sales |
| Membership | Not offered | Not applicable |
| Course | Not offered, and she will not do a long cohort | Not applicable |
| Client work | She will not do this | Not applicable |

## 3. What the numbers actually say

Using only the example figures: ads are about $1,400 a month; the template is about $1,470 a month; sponsors are about $4,000 to $6,000 a quarter, which is roughly $1,300 to $2,000 a month if you smooth them out. The template already matters as much as ads, and it is something she controls. Sponsor income is real, but it is lumpy. There is no data here for click-through rate on the template, or for how many newsletter readers already bought it.

## 4. Unanswered audience asks

- A client deliverable workflow (asked, no offer)
- A product launch week checklist (asked, no offer)
- Whether the current template still helps if someone already has a shop (asked, not answered in the listing)
- A live teardown of a product page (asked; she is willing to make short videos)

A long course would cover some of this, but she has already ruled that out.

## 5. The one next move

Ship a $19 product launch week checklist, sold next to the existing template, and film one 8-minute walkthrough that uses the checklist on a fictional product page.

Why this comes first: it answers a question people already ask, it stays in the "simple product she can update" lane, and it can be finished in a few weeks. A membership would add ongoing work she did not ask for. A deliverable-workflow guide is a good second product, but launch week is the clearer gap next to the template she already sells. "Done" means the checklist is for sale, the walkthrough is public, and the template page answers the "I already have a shop" question in one short paragraph.

## 6. Assumptions and unknowns

- All dollar amounts and audience sizes come from the example brief.
- We do not know conversion rate, refund rate, or how many buyers already have a shop.
- We do not know the cost of her time to update the template.
- Sponsor demand next quarter is unknown.
