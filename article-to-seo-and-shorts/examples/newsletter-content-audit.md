# Worked example with fictional inputs

The publication, URLs, results, channels, and dates in this example are invented. The [sample CSV](search-console.csv) represents a query-and-page report for `sc-domain:fieldnotes.example`, filtered to Web search for October 1 to 31, 2026.

## Published source

Field Notes ran a newsletter issue called “The sections we kept.” The editor reviewed recent issues before deciding which sections to keep for the next month. They put one section on each spreadsheet row, added the issue dates, and noted link clicks and recurring reader replies. They marked each section Keep, Adjust, or Retire. The issue shows how they used replies to keep an occasional reader Q&A even though it had few links. The archive lives at `https://dispatch.fieldnotes.example/archive/the-sections-we-kept`.

## Query choice

Use `newsletter content audit checklist` for an informational, task-oriented article. The sample logged 310 impressions and 12 clicks for that query on the site's general newsletter metrics guide. The searcher wants a checklist for reviewing sections and deciding what to change in the next issue. `Newsletter content audit` and the two question rows support the same job. The open-rate benchmark row has more impressions and signals a different question that the metrics guide already answers.

Create `https://fieldnotes.example/guides/newsletter-content-audit` for the decision workflow. Link to it from the existing metrics guide where readers are ready to turn those measures into an editorial decision.

## SEO outline

- **Page title.** A newsletter content audit checklist for your next issue
- **SEO title.** Newsletter Content Audit Checklist
- **Meta description.** Review recent newsletter issues, use clicks and reader replies as evidence, and decide which sections to keep, adjust, or retire.
- **Opening.** List the sections that repeat across recent issues. Write down what each section is meant to do and what readers did in response. Use that evidence to choose one change for the next issue.
- **Build your section inventory (H2).** Show a simple table with Issue, Section, Intended action, Evidence, and Next edit. Point readers to the [newsletter metrics guide](https://fieldnotes.example/guides/newsletter-metrics) for definitions of clicks and opens.
- **Decide what to keep, adjust, or retire (H2).** Use the reader Q&A example to show why a reply can be stronger evidence for a conversational section than a click. Give an example of adjusting a section by changing its placement for one issue and recording the result.
- **Put one decision into your next issue (H2).** Record the edit and the issue date. Link to the [editorial calendar template](https://fieldnotes.example/templates/newsletter-calendar) where the reader plans the next review.
- **FAQ from query rows.** “How often should you audit a newsletter?” Review after enough comparable issues to see a pattern. A monthly review is a reasonable starting point for a weekly newsletter. “What should you track in a newsletter audit?” Track the issue date, section purpose, the relevant clicks or replies, and the edit you will try next.
